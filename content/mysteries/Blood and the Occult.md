**Blood** (from Mephistopheles in [[Faust]]: *Blut ist ein ganz besondrer Saft*, "blood is a very special fluid"). For [[Rudolf Steiner]], in his 1906 lecture *The Occult Significance of Blood*, blood is *"the expression of the 'I,' or ego"* <span class="pg">p. 46</span>: the place where the self that can say *I* takes on a body.

### Core

**Signed in blood.** A name written in ink is a promise; a name written in blood is a surrender. Because the blood carries the *I*, whoever signs in it gives over not their word but themselves, which is why the devil will accept no other ink. *"If once I have thy name written in thy blood, then I can hold thee by that which above all sways a man"* <span class="pg">p. 43</span>.

**The blood is the I's own body.** Steiner borrows the Hermetic axiom, as above, so below: each part of the human being has a physical instrument, and the I's instrument is the blood. It stands between the inner world of images and the outer world of form: *"Just as by means of the brain the external world is experienced inwardly, so also by means of the blood this inner world is transformed into an outer expression in the body of man"* <span class="pg">p. 33</span>.

| Above              | Below          |
| ------------------ | -------------- |
| physical principle | physical body  |
| etheric body       | vital fluids   |
| astral body        | nervous system |
| "I"                | blood          |

**The ancestors live in it.** Blood is memory older than the person. In an earlier, dreamlike consciousness, Steiner claims, a son remembered his father's and grandfather's lives as his own, and all three felt themselves one *I*. Something of this returns whenever waking consciousness sinks, in trance or sleep: *"his forebears are active in his blood, and at such a time he dimly takes part in their remote life"* <span class="pg">p. 37</span>.

**Waking is a loss.** Once there was a *"hazy clairvoyance, from which the myths and legends originated"* <span class="pg">p. 41</span>. When kin stopped marrying kin, that ancestral, dreaming sight died, and the clear waking intellect was born in its place. Steiner does not call this simple progress: *"Our every-day objective consciousness is therefore the outcome of a destructive process"* <span class="pg">pp. 45–46</span>.

### Overview

Steiner gave the lecture in Berlin in 1906 and took his title from Mephistopheles' line in *Faust*. He reads the line as a spiritual truth rather than irony. Drawing on the Hermetic axiom *as above, so below*, he describes the human being as physical, etheric and astral bodies crowned by the *I*, and finds the blood to be the *I*'s physical expression. From there he moves through ancestry and inherited memory, the dreaming clairvoyance he believed humanity once had, and the waking intellect that replaced it. He ends where he began: whoever masters a person's blood masters the person.
